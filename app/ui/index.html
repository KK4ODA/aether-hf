Radio dial

?

The list starts as the frequency plan's proposals and is yours to edit: Add keeps a dial with a name (the same dial again renames it), Remove drops the chosen one, and it is saved beside the modem's configuration. Tune sets the radio to the chosen dial when it is keyed over CAT or rigctld; a serial line or a CM108 interface cannot tune, and the list is still a list. MHz
MHz
?

Disconnect finishes sending what is queued and then closes; on the receiving side it closes between the other station's bursts, and while calling the same button stops the call. Abort closes at once. Probe asks the station in Call whether it hears you, and how well, without a session: one frame, one answer, both directions of the path. Beacon puts this station's callsign on the air once, addressed to nobody, so anyone listening can report hearing you; the list beside it repeats it every 10 to 60 minutes while you are at the station, waiting for a clear channel, until set back to once — the line under the buttons says when the last went and the next is due, and the Log's Beacons filter lists every beacon sent and heard. Test session runs the field test with the station in Call: a probe, a message, a short burst at every mode and a file, all recorded — about five minutes of transmitting, sized to what the path can do, and a sidecar the project can learn from. The same button stops it. The other station only has to be listening; Help says how to contribute the result.

How to set your drive
  1. Press Set drive. It sends the real waveform in six-second bursts, five seconds apart, so the rig's ALC sees the peaks real traffic presents — a tune tone will not.
  2. Watch the radio's ALC meter. Raise the Transmit level above until the ALC just begins to move, then back it off until the ALC barely tickles. The level is live; the gaps between bursts let you see where it settled.
  3. If the ALC moves even with the level low, turn down the radio's USB (rear) data input gain and press Set drive again. That coarse gain and this fine slider both feed the ALC, and the rig's is usually the one set too high.
  4. Keep the peak in the green. Last transmit peak should read about −6 to −12 dB, never red — that is the modem's own headroom, before the rig.
  5. For more power, turn up the radio's power control, not the slider. A clean signal at flat ALC is what gets decoded. The waveform's average power is a fraction of its peaks, so a few watts average is normal and plenty; raise the rig's power and the clean drive scales up with it.

Key keys the radio with no audio, to check the PTT light. Tune is a steady tone for an antenna tuner — not for setting drive: a tone is a sine, and the data waveform's peaks land 6 to 7 dB higher, so drive set on the tone runs that far into ALC limiting on real traffic. Two gains feed the rig's ALC — this Transmit level and the radio's own USB (rear) data input gain — while the rig's power control sets how many watts the clean signal makes. All three keys are refused during a session, and Tune and Set drive while the channel is busy; every one of them is judged by the rules first. The level is saved when the slider is released.

Receiver

            

              ?
              

                Rate control is the rate controller's smoothed SNR and the margin it keeps
                above the mode's threshold. They hear you at is what the other station
                measured on your last burst, carried back in its acknowledgement. Mode
                confidence is how clearly the last frame's mode was read from its chips; below
                1.3 is a guess. The busy detector line gives every number behind its

                the channel busy. Host program and KISS say what is
                attached; Audio is the sound card's input.

    1Callsign and rules
    
                
    
                  ?
                  
    
                    Local and remote control follow the same rules: the
                    data segments of your class, a signal no wider than 2.8 kHz, all of it inside the
                    segment. Automatic is a station with nobody at the control point —
                    an unattended mailbox or gateway: it may transmit freely only inside the automatic
                    sub-bands of §97.221(b), and elsewhere only answer a station under local or remote
                    control, at 500 Hz or less (§97.221(c)). Aether judges every transmission before
                    keying and refuses what the rules do not allow; you remain the control operator.
                    The field log's grid, rig, power and antenna go into every recording, so a report
                    needs no follow-up; six grid characters let the log work out the path length.
                  
    
                
    
              
    
              
    
                
                
                Letters, digits, - and /; up to nine characters. An SSID (KK4ODA-1) or a suffix (KK4ODA/P) is fine, and a host program's own callsign is answered to as well.

  2. 
              
    
                
    2Radio interface
    
                
    
                  ?
                  
    
                    A serial port keys the radio through its RTS or DTR line, or by the radio's own
                    CAT command on the CAT port — which needs no second port and also reads the
                    dial frequency, which the rules need. The rate is the one in the radio's menu; an Icom
                    also needs its CI-V address (IC-7300 94, IC-705 A4, IC-9700 A2). Yaesu radios keyed
                    over CAT transmit from the DATA input. Hamlib's rigctld is the other way
                    to both. A radio's USB connection often shows two ports: Yaesu's Enhanced
                    port carries CAT and its Standard port the RTS line; Icom's single port
                    carries both. A DRA, URI or other CM108-class interface keys through its codec's own
                    GPIO pin (3 on those boards) and is listed by name, with no serial port. Choose
                    none for a VOX interface such as a SignaLink, or to listen only. Without CAT
                    or rigctld the radio cannot report its dial: say where it is on the
                    Session tab before transmitting.

                them filled in for it — you can change any of them afterwards.

  4. 
              
    
                
    4Modem settings
    
                
    
                  ?
                  
    
                    Both stations of a session use the same bandwidth; a call in the
                    other one is not heard, and changing it restarts the modem. Answer
                    only makes no call and sends no beacon; an unattended station is also
                    Automatic under Control in step 1, and the rules then decide where it may
                    answer (§97.221). Compression is deflate over the whole session, so
                    on a weak path one lost frame can spoil the rest. The Morse ID is off
                    by default — Aether's frames carry both callsigns, and whether that satisfies your
                    licence is your call; under the US rules an automatically keyed identifier goes no
                    faster than 20 wpm (§97.119(b)(1)), whatever is set here. Band edges
                    keeps a margin between your signal and a segment's edge for dial error and the
                    radio's own transmit chain.

  5. 
              
    
                
    5Application settings
    
                
    
                  ?
                  
    
                    Host programs use the VARA-compatible interface: point the program's
                    TNC settings at 127.0.0.1 and the port (the data port is the next number
                    up); the modem restarts when you change it. KISS programs connect to
                    a KISS over TCP port, 8100 by default — VARA HF's, so a program set up for
                    VARA needs no change. Each KISS frame goes out as its own transmission with no
                    acknowledgement, and only while no session is up; the program repeats what needs
                    repeating. Winlink Express and VarAC sessions use Host programs, not the
                    KISS port, which takes effect when saved, without a restart. Recording
                    notes are written into every recording that starts on its own, for the day
                    you leave the station listening and a friend calls in.

    Getting on the air
    
            
      
              
    1. Setup: callsign, the rules you operate under, the control and your license class, the radio interface and its devices, the receive level, save. A sound card, port or callsign change restarts the modem by itself.
      2. 
              
    2. Profiles, at the top of Setup: every setting under a name — one per radio or per place. Save as… keeps the current settings as a new one, picking another switches to it (a * means unsaved changes, and switching asks), Export… writes an .aetherprofile file to back up or carry to another computer and Import… brings one in.
      3. 
              
    3. The rules: the badge beside the dial says LEGAL, WARNING or TX BLOCKED for this station's widest transmission where the dial is now; click it for the reasoning. Aether keys nothing the rules refuse, and holds the link to the rungs they allow.
      4. 
              
    4. Session → Keying and drive: Key 1 s should light the rig's transmit indicator. Set your drive with Set drive (not the tune tone) — raise the level until the rig's ALC just moves, then back off so it barely tickles; if it moves even with the level low, turn down the radio's USB data input gain. Get power from the rig's power control, not the slider.
      5. 
              
    5. Session: type the other station's callsign and Connect. Leave the modem listening and it answers a call to your callsign whenever it comes; with recording on, every session is kept. Type in Send and press Enter: what you send stays in the box with its time and a ✓ once the other station has all of it (… on its way, ✗ if the session ended first).
      6. 
              
    6. Host programs: turn on Host programs in Setup, then point Winlink Express (a Vara HF session, TNC at 127.0.0.1 and the port, auto-launch off) or Pat (varahf) at it.
      7. 
              
    7. KISS programs: turn on KISS programs in Setup, then point an APRS or packet program's KISS over TCP settings at 127.0.0.1 and 8100 — VARA HF's KISS port, so one set up for VARA needs no change.
      8. 
            
    
          
    

          
          
    
            
    Beacons
    
            
      
              
    • Sending one: Beacon on the Session tab puts this station's callsign on the air once, on the tone floor, addressed to nobody — so anyone listening, on either bandwidth, can say they heard you.
      • 
              
    • Repeating it: the Repeat list beside the button sends one now and another every 10 to 60 minutes until you set it back to once. It waits for a clear channel and for any session, probe or test to finish, skips a beacon it could not send within two minutes rather than catching up, and stops when the modem restarts. It needs you at the station — local or remote control: a beacon may be automatically controlled only in the few segments of §97.203(d) (28.20–28.30 MHz and a handful above), and Aether does not beacon unattended.
      • 
              
    • What you sent: every beacon that goes on the air is in the Log (the Beacons filter), counted under Beacons sent on the Status tab, and the line under the Session tab's buttons says when the last went and when the next is due.
      • 
              
    • What you heard: a beacon heard is in the Log with its callsign and SNR, in Stations — the Beacons column counts them per station and says when the last one was, and the Beacons filter lists only those stations — and among the Diagnostics tab's frames. A beacon is never answered: a channel where every beacon drew a reply would be unusable.

    Running a test session
    
            
    
              A test session is how a contact becomes a measurement the project can use: a probe, a
              message, a file and a burst at every mode, recorded, in about five minutes. The other
              station only has to be listening — a person at the panel or an unattended station set to
              answer only.
            
    
            
      
              
    1. Agree the contact: a frequency, a time, and the bandwidth (Setup step 4; both stations must run the same one).
      2. 
              
    2. Setup step 1, once: your grid, rig, power and antenna. They go into every recording, so the report needs no follow-up.
      3. 
              
    3. Session: the other callsign in Call, then Test session. The line under the buttons follows each step; the log shows what each one found. The whole run stops at ten minutes on its own; Stop test ends it earlier, keeping what was learned.
      4. 
              
    4. Wait for complete. The recording is in the recordings folder — the Session tab's Open folder — as two files whose names end in _test: the .json is the report, the .wav the audio.
      5. 
              
    5. Contribute it: Contribute the last test session on the Session tab opens a pre-filled report. A GitHub account is needed (free); drag the .json into the text box, add the .wav zipped if you are happy to share the audio, and Submit.
      6. 
            
    
          
    

          
          
    
            
    Reading the panel
    
            
      
              
    • SNR is what this receiver measured on the last frame it found, referenced to 3 kHz. They hear you at is what the other station measured on your last burst, carried back in its acknowledgement.
      • 
              
    • Tuning is the carrier offset the receiver removed: positive means the other station is above your dial. A few hertz is nothing; a hundred is a dial to check.
      • 
              
    • Throughput counts application bytes both ways over the last thirty seconds; the Speed chart shows it against the mode's on-air rate. Mode is the rate the sender is using; the Diagnostics tab shows the rate controller's smoothed SNR and the margin it keeps.
      • 
              
    • Signal above noise and the channel chart are the busy detector's view of the channel; Receive level is the sound card's, which the Setup tab helps you set.
      • 
              
    • Signal analysis on the Diagnostics tab — constellation, spectrum and waterfall — can be undocked into a window of its own. Compact in the header shrinks the panel to the state and four readings for a small window. Every ? opens the explanation for the box it sits in.
