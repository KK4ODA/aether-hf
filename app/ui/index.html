The list starts as the frequency plan's proposals and is yours to edit: Add keeps a dial with a name (the same dial again renames it), Remove drops the chosen one, and it is saved beside the modem's configuration. Tune sets the radio to the chosen dial when it is keyed over CAT or rigctld; a serial line or a CM108 interface cannot tune, and the list is still a list. When the radio's own dial matches an entry, the list follows it.

Disconnect finishes sending what is queued and then closes. Abort does not. Probe asks the station in Call whether it hears you, and how well, without a session: one frame, one answer, both directions of the path. Beacon puts this station's callsign on the air once, addressed to nobody, so anyone listening can report hearing you. Test session runs the field test with the station in Call: a probe, a message, a file and a short burst at every mode, all recorded — about five minutes of transmitting, sized to what the path can do, and a sidecar the project can learn from. The same button stops it. The other station only has to be listening; Help says how to contribute the result.

Keying and drive

          

            
            
            
            
          

          

            Last transmit peak
            —
          

          

            
            
            −12 dB
          

          

            Key puts the radio into transmit with no audio: watch the rig
            and the interface's PTT light. Tune keys with a steady tone —
            that is what an antenna tuner needs, and it is not how to set drive.
            Set drive is: it sends the real waveform, at the fastest mode
            this station uses. A tone is a sine and the data waveform is not, so at the same
            average power the waveform's peaks land about 6 to 7 dB higher — and it is peaks
            the ALC answers to. Drive set on the tone is that far into limiting on real
            traffic, which smears the signal and costs far more than it saves. So: press
            Set drive: four bursts of six seconds, five seconds apart. Watch
            the ALC during a burst and move the slider — the level is live, so the meter
            answers at once — then use the gap to see where it settled. Back the level off
            until the ALC barely moves. The level follows the slider while either plays. All three are
            refused during a session, and both transmissions are refused while the channel
            is busy. The level is saved when the slider is released.

    1. Callsign
    
              
    
                
                
                Letters, digits, - and /; up to nine characters. An SSID (KK4ODA-1) or a suffix (KK4ODA/P) is fine. A host program that names its own callsign is answered to as well.

Six is worth typing: the field log uses it to work out the path length between stations, and the four-character square can only do that to within half a square. Optional, like the rest of this.

                A serial port keys the radio through its RTS or DTR line, or by the radio's own
                CAT command on the CAT port — which needs no second port and also
                records the dial frequency. The rate is the one in the radio's menu; an Icom
                also needs its CI-V address (IC-7300 94, IC-705 A4, IC-9700 A2). Yaesu radios
                keyed over CAT transmit from the DATA input. Hamlib's rigctld is the
                other way to both. A radio's USB connection often shows two ports: Yaesu's
                Enhanced port carries CAT and its Standard port the RTS line;
                Icom's single port carries both. A DRA, URI or other CM108-class interface
                keys through its codec's own GPIO pin (3 on those boards) and is listed here
                by name, with no serial port. Choose none for a VOX interface such
                as a SignaLink, or to listen only.

Getting on the air

          
    
            
  1. Setup: callsign, the radio interface and its three devices, the receive level, save. A sound card, port or callsign change restarts the modem by itself.
    2. 
            
  2. Profiles, at the top of Setup: every setting under a name — one per radio or per place. Save as… keeps the current settings as a new one, picking another switches to it (a * means unsaved changes, and switching asks), Export… writes an .aetherprofile file to back up or carry to another computer and Import… brings one in. A device the other computer does not have is shown as not on this computer until you choose one.
    3. 
            
  3. Session → Keying and drive: Key for 1 s should light the rig's transmit indicator; the Tune tone with the level slider sets the drive so the ALC barely moves.
    4. 
            
  4. Session: type the other station's callsign and Connect. Leave the modem listening and it answers a call to your callsign whenever it comes; with recording on, every session is kept.
    5. 
            
  5. Host programs: turn on Host programs in Setup, then point Winlink Express (a Vara HF session, TNC at 127.0.0.1 and the port, auto-launch off) or Pat (varahf) at it.
    6. 
          

        


        

          
Running a test session

          

            A test session is how a contact becomes a measurement the project can use: a
            probe, a message, a file and a burst at every mode, recorded, in about five
            minutes. The other station only has to be listening — a person at the panel or
            an unattended station set to answer only.
          

          
    
            
  1. Agree the contact: a frequency, a time, and the bandwidth (Setup step 4; both stations must run the same one).
    2. 
            
  2. Setup step 1, once: your grid, rig, power and antenna. They go into every recording, so the report needs no follow-up.
    3. 
            
  3. Session: the other callsign in Call, then Test session. The line under the buttons follows each step; the log shows what each one found. The message and the file are sized to what the path can do, and the whole run stops at ten minutes on its own. Stop test ends it earlier, keeping what was learned.
    4. 
            
  4. Wait for complete. The recording is in the recordings folder (Help menu › Open the configuration folder), two files whose names end in _test: the .json is the report, the .wav the audio.
    5. 
            
  5. Contribute it: the Contribute the last test session button on the Session tab copies a link to a pre-filled report. Paste it into your browser (a GitHub account is needed, free), drag the .json into the text box, add the .wav zipped if you are happy to share the audio, and Submit.
    6. 
          

          

            The Session tab has the Contribute the last test session button, the
            recordings folder, and an Open folder control — everything for the
            run-test-then-share workflow is there.
          

        


        

          
Reading the panel

          
    
            
  • SNR is what this receiver measured on the last frame it found, referenced to 3 kHz. They hear you at is what the other station measured on your last burst, carried back in its acknowledgement.
    • 
            
  • Tuning is the carrier offset the receiver removed: positive means the other station is above your dial. A few hertz is nothing; a hundred is a dial to check.
    • 
            
  • Throughput counts application bytes both ways over the last thirty seconds. Mode is the rate the sender is using; the Diagnostics tab shows the rate controller's smoothed SNR and the margin it keeps above the mode's threshold.
    • 
            
  • Signal above noise and the level chart are the busy detector's view of the channel; Receive level is the sound card's, which the Setup tab helps you set.
    • 
            
  • Stations lists everyone whose frame carried a callsign; Compact in the header shrinks the panel to the state and four readings for a small window.
