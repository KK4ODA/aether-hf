# Step 4 at 2300 Hz: WC4Y's Test of 2026-10-23

Reports [11](11-step-4-first-air.md) to [14](14-wc4y-beta118.md) read protocol 8's sessions at
500 Hz. This one is the first 2300 Hz session on the air under granted slots, and the first at
2300 Hz since 2026-09-25. The author watched the waterfall and wrote: "Aether was trying to push
speeds that could not be sustained well, as they looked in the noise. Perhaps it still decoded?
Based on my experience with VARA HF, I know VARA would have used a lower rung." §5 answers that.
§7 is the busy detector, which the author asked about with a screenshot of the Status tab.

* **When and where**: 3.590 MHz, 2300 Hz, 2026-10-23 00:52:31Z to 00:57:53Z (20:52 EDT on the
  22nd).
* **Who**:
  * **WC4Y** was the master: he ran a Test calling KK4ODA-1. IC-7300 keyed by Icom CI-V,
    20–25 W, 230 ft EFHW, EM73VU.
  * **KK4ODA-1** was the slave: the author's FTDX10, Yaesu CAT, 50 W, T2D, EM73, the mini PC.
    The author's note: "80m, CW in passband".
* **Builds**: v0.2.0-beta.120 at both ends (link protocol 11), with ADR-0088 (the busy detector
  holds through a session, starts clean, and is recorded) and ADR-0089 (a capture skip is put
  back as silence). WC4Y moved his station from 500 to 2300 Hz 21 s before the Test.
* **The files**: both stations uploaded their side with audio at 00:58Z: WC4Y's
  `20261023-005231_WC4Y_KK4ODA-1_test` and KK4ODA-1's `20261023-005251_KK4ODA-1_WC4Y`, with
  each daemon's log and diagnostic bundle. The audio and files are not committed.

## 0. Summary

**What happened.**

* **The Test completed** in 312.8 s of its 600 s: probe, call, a 512-byte message at 55 bit/s,
  a 768-byte file at 61 bit/s, the ladder (rungs 5–12), disconnect, both identifiers.
* **Inside the session the granted slots held.** The stations never radiated together. The least
  clear air was 0.634 s. No forfeit, no late drop. Every answer reached the air 89–134 ms after
  its slot opened, at least 216 ms inside its limit.
* **The path**: KK4ODA-1 read WC4Y's data at −4.0 to +1.6 dB (median −1.6). WC4Y read the answers
  at −9.8 to −3.0 dB (median −5.1), every one on the floor.

**The author was right: the link sent faster than the path carried** (§5).

* Most of the data went at **rung 7, BPSK ⅓ on OFDM** (349 bit/s on the air). WC4Y sent 58
  frames at that rung. KK4ODA-1 never detected 20 of them. It decoded 7 on their own. Another 9
  got through only by combining two to four copies. Two frames were given up and sent again at
  rung 2.
* **The tone rungs decoded every frame sent**: rung 4 (tone100-105) 5 of 5, rung 5
  (tone100-153) 4 of 4, rung 2 2 of 2. The best moment on the author's speed chart, about
  200 bit/s, is rung 4's five frames.
* **Why the link stayed on rung 7.** On the 2300 Hz air the rate controller holds rung 7 to its
  AWGN threshold plus at most 1 dB, whatever it has learned (ADR-0013 §4). It leaves rung 7 only
  after two failed bursts in a row, and a burst that combining rescued counts as clean. A frame
  never detected counts for nothing. So KK4ODA-1 recommended rung 7 for the first 135 s of data.
* **What a slower rung would have done**: rung 5 sends the message in one transmission. On the
  scenario harness, with this path as a new scenario, removing the cap took the draw that
  repeated the air's pattern from 80 to 173 bit/s on the message, and 95 to 171 on the file.
  The other three draws started at rung 5 and did not change.

**Three other findings.**

* **KK4ODA-1's repeating beacon went out over WC4Y's closing identifier** (§3). It keyed 10 ms
  after KK4ODA-1's own identifier ended. WC4Y's identifier was due 0.8 s later and went out
  entirely under the beacon. The engine holds its own calls and probes until the other station's
  identifier is over; the daemon's beacon does not ask it.
* **The ladder's rung 9 is recorded as 3 of 4 decoded. KK4ODA-1 decoded none** (§6). Rung 9's
  slot went silent, and the next answer repeated rung 8's report, which the master took for rung
  9's.
* **The busy detector worked at both ends** (§7). KK4ODA-1's floor sat at the channel's noise
  all session, the fault report 14 found is gone, and its busy flag followed WC4Y's
  transmissions. One defect: WC4Y's gain reference learned the radio's levels in the first
  seconds, while the IC-7300's audio was still near silence, and read the radio's gain as 13–26 dB
  high for the whole session.

**What I would change, ranked** (details in §11):

1. *Rate control.* On the 2300 Hz air, remove the 1 dB cap on rung 7's margin and the rule
   that needs two failures in a row to leave it, as the 500 Hz air already has neither. It
   deletes code. First judge it on the harness over every 2300 Hz scenario.
2. *Rate control, the larger question.* The rate controller has been adjusted by about ten
   ADRs, and this session shows it cannot see what a rung costs. By the author's rule, a part
   that needs a third fix gets a review, not another patch.
3. *Daemon, a bug.* A beacon (repeating or pressed) and a KISS datagram wait for the engine's
   closing hold, as calls and probes do.
4. *Engine.* A measurement whose slot went silent is measured again, not credited with the next
   report.
5. *Busy detector: no patch*, by the author's decision. The gain reference's start goes into
   the evidence for the review question.
6. *Small.* "Held for busy" counts loop passes, not transmissions held.

### 5.3 Why the controller held rung 7

Four rules together. Each was added for a reason the link bench measured.

1. **The cap** (ADR-0013 §4, measured again in ADR-0014 §4). On the 2300 Hz air the first OFDM
   rung is held to its AWGN threshold plus at most `floor_margin_db` = 1 dB, however wide the
   learned margin. Rung 7's AWGN threshold is −3.2 dB, so it stays on offer while the smoothed
   SNR is above −2.2 dB. KK4ODA-1 read rung 7's frames at a median −2.0 dB. Rung 7's 10 % point
   on ITU Moderate is +4.2 dB, and +1.8 dB on Poor.
2. **"Once"** (the same ADR). One failed burst on that rung does not drop the link to the floor;
   two in a row do.
3. **A burst rescued by combining counts as clean.** The engine counts a frame as decoded when
   its payload arrives, whether it decoded alone or with earlier copies. So the message's bursts
   read failure, clean, failure, clean, failure, clean, clean, and "two in a row" never came.
   On the file, the second and third copies failed twice in a row, and the recommendation fell
   to rung 5 at 198.78 s.
4. **A frame never detected counts for nothing.** The controller learns from the frames in
   front of it (`_observe`). A frame the header said was coming but the receiver never found is
   not one of them, though the header states the count exactly since protocol 8.

The call started it. KK4ODA-1 read the call at −0.3 dB and said so in its acceptance; with the
cap, `first_mode` starts at rung 7 on a reading of −0.7 dB or better. Without the cap the first
burst goes at rung 5.

## 6. The ladder credited rung 9 with rung 8's report

* Rung 8's four measurement frames (273.33 s): KK4ODA-1 decoded three, and its answer reported
  frames 0, 1 and 3 (`REPORT` and bits 1, 2, 4).
* Rung 9's transmission (285.44 s): KK4ODA-1 missed the header and failed the three frames it
  detected. Silent slot.
* The master's poll (294.93 s), an ordinary header: never detected. Silent slot.
* The master's poll on the floor (300.19 s): decoded. KK4ODA-1 had heard no new measurement,
  so its answer carried the report it held, rung 8's. The master took it for rung 9's: "rung 9:
  3/4 decoded".
* So the Test's report says highest passed 9; it was 8. And rung 9 counted as a pass, so the
  ladder ran three more rungs (10–12) before three failures in a row stopped it; it would have
  stopped after 11.
* ADR-0076 already keeps a silent slot's SNR out of the ladder ("its frames counted, its SNR not
  the rung's"). The frames counted are not the rung's either when the slave never read the
  measurement. The master cannot tell the two cases apart. The safe rule is to measure the rung
  again (`_ask_again`, which ADR-0077 uses for a measurement that never reached the air).
  Change 4.

## 7. The busy detector

The author's decision of 2026-10-22: no more patches; watch a few sessions, and if it needs
fixing again, give it the full review. This is the first session on beta.120, with the busy
detector's state and readings in both sidecars.

### 7.1 KK4ODA-1: the floor held

KK4ODA-1's busy readings (4 a second), split by what was on the air:

| | readings | busy | level | floor | gain read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KK4ODA-1 keyed, or deaf after | 402 | 15 % | −24.8 dBFS | −24.6 | 0.0 dB |
| WC4Y's frames arriving | 569 | 72 % | −23.1 | −26.4 (to −34.5) | −2.1 (to −9.9) |
| neither | 240 | 28 % | −24.6 | −24.4 | +0.2 |

* **The floor sat at the noise.** −24.4 dBFS in the quiet is report 14's −31 dBFS noise in a
  500 Hz channel, plus 6.6 dB for a channel 4.6 times as wide. Report 14's fault (the floor
  drawn at the other station's signal once the hold ran out) did not recur: ADR-0088 holds.
* **Busy while WC4Y transmitted, 72 % of the time.** A frame decoded, the passband peaked by a
  tone frame, and energy over a threshold drawn lower by the radio's gain. The 28 % in the quiet
  is the 2 s hangover after each frame.
* **The FTDX10's AGC did act on WC4Y's tone frames.** In the audio, the passband's total power
  rose 2.2 dB under a tone frame while the median noise bin fell 1.3 dB (up to about 7 dB when a
  tone faded up). The gain reference read that cut, and the floor was drawn under it (ADR-0078).
  That is the red "busy: level" in the author's chart: right, and for the right reason.
* **No CW carrier in the recording.** The steady peaks at 1310–1410 Hz are WC4Y's floor
  tones. The CW the author noted was outside the recording, or too weak to show.

### 7.2 WC4Y: the floor held; the gain reference did not

| | readings | busy | level | floor | gain read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WC4Y keyed, or deaf after | 858 | 16 % | −21.3 dBFS | −20.7 | +20.7 dB |
| KK4ODA-1's frames arriving | 302 | 83 % | −18.5 | −20.7 | +19.6 |
| neither | 130 | 44 % | −20.7 | −20.7 | +19.8 |

* **The start was clean this time.** The daemon started at 00:51:32.4Z. At +2.5 s the busy
  detector's first floor was −50.2 dBFS: the IC-7300's USB audio was still near silence. Two
  seconds later the floor was −28.3 and the busy flag cleared. Before ADR-0088 it stayed busy
  for a minute after every start.
* **The gain reference learned that silence.** ADR-0088 keeps the reference deaf for the
  capture's first second; this codec was quiet for more than two. The reference took its levels
  on quiet from the silence and then read the radio's gain as 13–26 dB above them, all session,
  and it was not back by the end. A reading above 0 dB is not used, so it did no harm here. But
  for the session ADR-0078 was off at WC4Y: a real AGC cut of less than about 17 dB would read as
  none.
* **The call waited 2.0 s for a clear channel**: the hangover after the probe's answer. The
  busy flag was right.
* **"Held for busy" says 962.** The counter adds one for every pass of the run loop while a
  transmission waits, not one per transmission held; WC4Y's call and probe waited 2.1 s in all. The panel's tooltip says "Transmissions held back for a
  busy channel". Change 6.

### 7.3 What this says for the review question

* The part that was patched eight times, the floor, held at both ends on its first session since
  ADR-0088.
* The new defect is in the gain reference's start, the same kind of fault as report 14's (state
  learned before the radio's audio means anything). It cost nothing in this session.
* My reading: not yet the trigger for the full review, but a second count toward it. If the
  next sessions show the reference's start doing harm, that is the review. The author's call.

## 11. What to change

1. **Rate control, 2300 Hz: remove the floor-boundary cap and "once".**
   * The 2300 Hz air would treat rung 7 like any other rung, by the learned margin, as the
     500 Hz air has always done.
   * Removes `floor_margin_db` (`PhyTiming`, `RateController._margin`, the boundary branch of
     `_step_down` and `_boundary_failures`), `WIDE_FLOOR_MARGIN_DB` and the daemon's setting, in
     both suites. Adds nothing.
   * Judged on the harness, not the link bench: every 2300 Hz scenario, three draws each,
     against beta.120, with criteria registered first (no session lost, no scenario slower than
     its range in `scenarios.csv`, the new scenario's message rate up). §5.5 is one draw.
   * Model first, no wire change.
2. **Rate control: a review, not a patch.**
