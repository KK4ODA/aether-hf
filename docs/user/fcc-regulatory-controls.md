# FCC regulatory controls

Aether HF judges every transmission against the rules of the station's licence before the radio
is keyed, and refuses what the rules do not allow. This page explains what it checks under the
United States profile (47 CFR Part 97), how it reads the rules where they can be read more than
one way, and what you need to tell it.

> **Aether does not replace the control operator.** You remain responsible for every
> transmission your station makes (§97.103, §97.105). Aether's checks are a safeguard against
> mistakes — a dial turned mid-session, a setting forgotten — not a legal opinion. If Aether and
> the rules disagree, the rules win; please report it.

The rules were read from the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, Title 47 current to
2026-09-23 (Part 97 last amended 2026-02-13); the 6 m entries on 2026-09-25, from the text current
to 2026-09-24. The sections that matter:

**warning**, never a refusal.

## What you tell Aether (Setup, step 1)

* **Rules** — *United States — FCC Part 97*, or *None — I check every transmission myself*.
  Until you choose, nothing is transmitted. (A configuration from before the rules were added, beta.60, is
  brought forward once: an FCC-assigned callsign gets the US profile, and an answer-only station
  gets *Automatic* control. Nothing else is assumed.)
* **Control** — how the station is controlled:
  * **Local** — you are at the radio (the control point).
  * **Remote** — you control it through a control link, from somewhere else.
  * **Automatic** — nobody is at a control point: an unattended mailbox, gateway or answering
    station.

  Aether never works this out from the network or from a host program (apart from that one
  migration of an old file); only you know it. Its own
  automation — ARQ, retries, rate adaptation, acknowledgements — is not "automatic control": the
  control mode is how the *station* is controlled.
* **License class** — Technician, General, Advanced, Amateur Extra (or Novice). Never assumed.
* **Sideband** — USB unless your radio sends the modem's audio on LSB.

Setup step 4 has the **band-edge margin** (50 Hz by default: room for your dial's error and the
radio's own transmit chain), **band-plan guidance** (on by default) and whether to **log every
automatic transmission**, not only the refused ones.

## Where your signal actually is

Compliance depends on the RF your signal occupies, not on the dial and not on a mode's name.
Aether's waveform sits in the audio passband around 1500 Hz, so on **USB** it is on the air from
*dial + lowest audio* to *dial + highest audio*; on **LSB** it is below the dial, mirrored.

What each waveform occupies was measured from the transmitter itself and is kept in the daemon
(`core/aetherd/data/occupancy.json`, generated by `tools/make_occupancy.py`):

| Waveform | Occupies (the wider reading of §97.3(a)(8)) |
|---|---|
| Tone floor (both bandwidths, rungs 0–1) | about 550 Hz, 1225–1775 Hz of audio |
| 2300 Hz air, 50-baud tones (rungs 2–3) | about 1.1 kHz |
| 2300 Hz air, 100-baud tones (rungs 4–5) | about 2.2 kHz |
| 2300 Hz air, OFDM (rungs 6–19) | about 2.5 kHz, 240–2760 Hz of audio |
| 500 Hz air, four-tone rungs (2–3) | about 610 Hz |
| 500 Hz air, OFDM (rungs 4–14) | 690–710 Hz |

Everything is under the 2.8 kHz data limit. The "500 Hz" setting is a *nominal* width: the
signal it produces measures more than 500 Hz.

**Bandwidth, read conservatively.** §97.3(a)(8) defines bandwidth as the width of a band outside
of which the mean power is at least 26 dB below the mean power within it. Read as a ratio of
powers, that is the band holding all but 1/398 of the power; read as a spectral level, it is where
the spectrum falls 26 dB below its average within the band (what a spectrum analyser's "−26 dB
bandwidth" shows). The tone floor is 434–445 Hz one way and 551 Hz the other. Aether uses **the
wider of the two**, because the rule can be read either way.

A transmission is allowed when the whole signal, with the margin at both ends, is inside a
segment where the rules allow it:

    RF low  − margin  ≥  segment low
    RF high + margin  ≤  segment high

A dial that looks right can still put the top of the signal past the segment's edge. Aether says
by how much: *TX BLOCKED: signal extends 260 Hz beyond the FCC data segment*, with the RF range.

**The dial.** Keyed over CAT, `rigctld` or FLRig, Aether reads the dial from the radio before each
transmission. Keyed over a serial line, a CM108 interface or VOX, the radio cannot say, so the
Session tab asks you: *Dial is at …* — set it whenever you move the dial. With no dial known,
nothing is transmitted. Keyed by a host program (VarAC's way, Setup step 2 *host program*), Aether
can read no dial either: choosing it sets the rules to *None*, and if you choose a profile anyway,
set *Dial is at …* as for any radio that cannot report its dial.

## The data segments and your privileges

Data is allowed only in the lower parts of the bands (§97.305(c)) and only inside your class's
privileges (§97.301):

| Band | Data segment | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 160 m | 1.800–2.000 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 80 m | 3.500–3.600 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 60 m | the four channels, and 5351.5–5366.5 kHz | §97.303(h)(3) |
| 40 m | 7.000–7.125 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 30 m | 10.100–10.150 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 20 m | 14.000–14.150 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 17 m | 18.068–18.110 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 15 m | 21.000–21.200 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 12 m | 24.890–24.930 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 10 m | 28.000–28.300 MHz | §97.305(c) |
| 6 m | 50.100–54.000 MHz | §97.305(c)(4)(i), (ii) |

General and Advanced privileges start 25 kHz into several of these (for example 14.025 MHz on
20 m); Technicians have CW only on 80, 40 and 15 m (§97.307(f)(9)) and CW, RTTY and data on
28.000–28.300 MHz. **6 m** is Technician, General, Advanced and Extra alike, all of 50–54 MHz
(§97.301(a)), with no Novice privileges; 50.0–50.1 MHz is CW only, so a data signal must be all of
it above 50.1 MHz. On 6 m §97.307(f)(5) authorizes 20 kHz and (f)(2) holds a non-phone emission to
the width of a communications-quality phone emission of the same modulation type — a single-
sideband voice channel, read as HF's 2.8 kHz — so the narrower decides; Aether's widest signal is
2.52 kHz. §97.313(b)'s 1.5 kW is the only power limit there.

The Diagnostics tab's **Rules** card lists, for your class, the exact dial ranges where the
widest waveform and the tone floor fit, on your sideband and with your margin.

## Local and remote control

The same rules for both: the data segments of your class, 2.8 kHz, the whole signal inside the
segment. Near a segment edge Aether keeps the link on the rungs that fit — the badge reads
**WARNING** and says which — instead of refusing the session.

## Automatic control (§97.221)

An automatically controlled station may transmit data:

* anywhere inside the **automatic-control sub-bands** of §97.221(b) — 3.585–3.600, 7.100–7.105,
  10.140–10.150, 14.0950–14.0995, 14.1005–14.112, 18.105–18.110, 21.090–21.100, 24.925–24.930 and
  28.120–28.189 MHz, **and all of 6 m** ("the 6 m or shorter wavelength bands") — as long as the
  whole signal is inside one of them;
* elsewhere (inside a data segment) **only when responding to interrogation** by a station under
  local or remote control, and **only with a signal of 500 Hz or less** (§97.221(c)).

Aether cannot check that the station calling it is under local or remote control, so a session
another station started is treated as interrogation. Every Aether emission measures more than
500 Hz by the conservative reading of bandwidth (the tone floor is 551 Hz), so **an automatic
station runs only inside the (b) sub-bands**. There, the link may use every rung whose whole
signal fits inside the sub-band from where the dial is — near a sub-band's edge, only the
narrower ones. Aether never upgrades an automatic station to a wider waveform than the rules
allow where it is, whatever the other station offers.

An automatic station does not operate on 60 m at all: §97.221(c) excepts the 60 m channels, and
whether that reaches the 60 m segment too is not clear, so Aether reads it as excepting both.

Tests at the control point — the tune tone, drive bursts, the keying test — are judged as a
locally controlled station's: somebody is at the radio when they are pressed.

## Beacons and identification

* A **beacon** — one frame with your callsign, addressed to nobody, on the tone floor — is
  judged like any other transmission. **A station under automatic control sends none** — not
  the Session tab's, not a repeating one (the *Repeat* list, `beacon.every`), and not one a host
  program asks for (VarAC's CQ, which reaches Aether as `CQFRAME`) — because §97.203(d) lets a beacon be automatically
  controlled only on 28.20–28.30 MHz, 50.06–50.08 MHz (inside 6 m's CW-only 50.0–50.1 MHz, so
  never for Aether's data) and a few segments above 6 m (ADR-0024). An answer-only station sends
  no beacon at all.
* A beacon carries no Morse identifier. Its frame carries your callsign.

### The Morse identifier

* **When it goes** (ADR-0075, as VARA HF does it): once a session, at the end. It is the last
  thing each station sends for the session, however the session ends.
  * In a normal close the station that answers the goodbye sends its identifier right behind
    the answer, and the station that said goodbye sends its own after that.
  * After an abort or a lost path, each station sends its identifier on its own, after listening
    for a clear channel for up to 15 s.
  * Nothing during a session sends one, and neither do calls, acceptances, probes, probe answers,
    beacons or KISS frames.
  * A call or probe that leads to no session is identified on its own, 30 s after.
* **Who sends it.** A US callsign always does, whatever *Morse ID* says in Setup and whatever a
  host program's `CWID OFF` says. VARA HF forces it for US callsigns too. Any other callsign
  sends it only if *Morse ID* is ticked; it is off by default. *US* is decided from the callsign
  the identifier sends, by where the station operates:
  * the prefixes AA–AL, K, N and W are US;
  * an SSID (`-1`), `-T`/`-R`, and `/P`, `/M`, `/MM`, `/QRP` or a bare digit change nothing;
  * a reciprocal prefix in front decides (`W4/G4ABC` is US, `VE3/KK4ODA` is not), as does a
    location after the callsign (`G4ABC/W4` is US, `KK4ODA/VE3` is not). §97.119(g) puts the
    indicator in front, and after the callsign for a Canadian licence; both are read.
* **What it sends.** The callsign without its SSID: `KK4ODA-1` goes out as `KK4ODA`, and a
  `-T` or `-R` is dropped too. §97.119(a) asks for the station's assigned call sign, and an SSID
  is not part of it. Sent, `-1` was a hyphen and a digit, 2.3 s of a 6.3 s identifier. A `/`
  part is kept: a reciprocal or location indicator (`W4/G4ABC`, `G4ABC/W4`) is part of how the
  station identifies (§97.119(g)), and `/P` or `/M` is the operator's choice.
* **How it is sent.** It is judged as a CW emission at the dial it goes out on, a 1 500 Hz tone.
  It goes at **20 wpm at most**, §97.119(b)(1)'s limit for an identifier sent by an automatic
  device. A faster setting is held to 20, and Setup step 4 and the log say so.

### What identifies a long session

§97.119(a) asks for the call sign at the end of each communication **and at least every 10
minutes during it**. What Aether and the host programs send, as of this release:

* **Aether's own frames.** Calls, acceptances, probes, probe answers, beacons and KISS datagrams
  carry the callsigns. They are packed in Aether's own 6-bit code (`pack_callsign` in the air
  interface), not Baudot, AMTOR or ASCII. Session frames (data, acknowledgements and the
  goodbye) carry none.
* **Aether's Morse identifier**: at the end only. Nothing in Aether identifies a session between
  its call and its end.
* **VarAC**: its *Call ID interval* setting sends `DE <your callsign>` as chat text on its own
  schedule. Aether carries that text as data, inside its own frames, and compressed if
  *Compress* is on (off by default).
* **Winlink (B2F, Winlink Express, Pat, RMS Trimode)**: the callsigns are exchanged at the start
  of the session. Nothing after that identifies it.
* **The panel's sessions and Tests**: nothing after the call.

**So a session longer than ten minutes is not identified in Morse between its call and its end.**
Only VarAC's *Call ID interval* text identifies one in between, and only if you set it.

Whether text or callsigns inside Aether's frames meet §97.119(b)(3) is an open question. That
rule names "a RTTY emission using a specified digital code". Aether's air interface is publicly
documented (§97.309(a)(4) allows such a technique to carry a specified code). But its callsign
packing is not one of the specified codes. PACTOR and ARDOP put the callsigns in their own
frames and treat that as identification. No FCC or ARRL statement on the point was found.
Aether adds nothing to fill the gap. If your sessions run past ten minutes, how you identify
during them is your call.

## 60 m

* **Channels** (centres 5332, 5348, 5373 and 5405 kHz): a data emission's carrier goes 1.5 kHz
  below the centre — that is the **dial**, on USB — and Aether's waveform, centred 1.5 kHz above
  its dial, then sits on the centre. Aether accepts the dial within 10 Hz of the carrier
  frequency. A CW emission's carrier goes on the centre. The signal stays within 2.8 kHz.
* **The segment**, 5351.5–5366.5 kHz: data up to 2.8 kHz, 9.15 W ERP.
* Only phone, RTTY, data and CW: the tune tone is refused on 60 m — tune up on another band.
* Power: 100 W ERP on the channels, 9.15 W ERP in the segment (§97.313(i)); Aether warns when the
  power you gave in Setup is higher (ERP includes your antenna's gain, which Aether cannot know).

## If the dial or the settings change mid-session

Aether judges every burst, so a dial turned out of the segment, a sideband or control mode
changed, stops the session at the next transmission: the refusal is logged with its reason, and
Aether does **not** send a disconnect frame there — it would be as unlawful as the data. The other
station times out. The link's ceiling is recomputed at once.

The rules settings take effect when Setup is saved: a change made on the Setup tab and not saved
is not in force, and the panel says so on every tab until it is saved or discarded.

## Legal, Warning, TX blocked

The badge in the header (and beside the dial on the Session tab) is the verdict for your
station's **widest** transmission at the dial it is on now:

* **Legal** — allowed, and nothing to add.
* **Warning** — allowed, with something you should know: outside the band plan's digital areas,
  on a beacon frequency, a power limit, or the link held to its narrower rungs near an edge.
* **TX blocked** — not allowed; nothing will be transmitted. The reason says what to change.
* **No rules** — you chose no profile; Aether checks nothing.

Click it for the reasoning: the RF range, the segment and its rule (linked to the e-CFR), your
control and class, and the band-plan note. The Diagnostics tab's **Rules** card shows everything
the policy knows — the ceiling on the link, the last decision it made, and the dial ranges where
each waveform fits. The Log tab's **Rules** filter lists every decision it logged.

## Logs

Every refused transmission is one line in the daemon's log, with everything an on-air test needs
to show why: the decision, the rule, the callsign, the dial and where it came from, the sideband,
the RF range, the bandwidth, the mode, the emission, the control, who began the exchange, the
session state, the class and the reason. With **log every automatic transmission** on, an
automatic station also logs the basis for each transmission it was allowed to make.

## Other countries

The rules live in a profile (`core/aetherd/data/regulatory/us-fcc-part97.json`), and another
administration's rules are another profile. Until one exists for your country, choose **None** —
and check every transmission yourself.
